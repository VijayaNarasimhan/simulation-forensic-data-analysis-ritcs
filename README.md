**Simulation: Financial Forensics Data Analysis Case Study**

**Project Overview**

This is an independently prepared synthetic financial-forensics case study involving Revolution IT Consulting & Services Ltd., a fictional IT consulting and services company suspected, within the simulated scenario, of recording potentially fictitious revenue in the period preceding a proposed IPO.

The project demonstrates the application of structured financial and transactional data analysis to identify potential indicators of revenue manipulation and determine areas requiring further investigation.

All company names, customers, employees, transactions, relationships and supporting records used in this case are fictional or stipulated solely for this exercise. The project does not concern or allege misconduct by any real entity or individual.

**Objectives**
- Screen revenue transactions for unusual patterns and potential exceptions.
- Examine customer concentration and stipulated candidate related-party relationships.
- Review transaction timing, approval thresholds and employee-authorisation patterns.
- Analyse contract information, outstanding receivables and potential employee incentive conflicts.
- Document findings, limitations and further investigative procedures.

**Scope and Methodology**

The exercise uses synthetic transaction data and supporting customer, employee, contract, receivables and ESOP records. The procedures include duplicate testing, creator/approver hierarchy checks, transaction-timing screening, revenue-trend analysis, near-threshold analysis, customer-concentration and related-party analysis, contract-tracker review, receivables analysis and ESOP-related-person screening.

**Summary of Simulated Results**

The analysis identified potential exceptions requiring further review, including transaction-timing flags, concentration of recorded revenue among candidate related parties, transactions within the selected approval-threshold band and outstanding receivables associated with flagged transactions.

The results are screening observations from synthetic data. They do not establish that fraud occurred. Further corroboration of customer relationships, service delivery, approval evidence and subsequent settlement would be required to reach defensible investigative conclusions.

**Project Files**

**Preliminary Assessment and Data Analysis Plan:** scenario, initial considerations, hypotheses and planned procedures.
**Raw Extract:** synthetic source data used for the exercise.
**Data Analysis Workings:** analytical procedures, calculations and identified exceptions.
**Results and Annexures:** written findings and supporting schedules.
**Disclaimer:** scope and limitations of the synthetic case study.

**Important Limitations**

This is an educational simulation, not a real-world investigation, audit engagement or professional opinion. Some facts are stipulated as starting conditions for the scenario. Analytical flags require validation and corroboration; unusual patterns alone do not establish fraud.

**Author's Note**

The purpose of this exercise is to demonstrate an interest in financial forensics and the application of accounting, analytical and investigative reasoning to a structured financial-integrity problem.
